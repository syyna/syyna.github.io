# Starseer's Ruin
[Goodreads Review](https://www.goodreads.com/review/show/8541206555)

Tchaikovsky takes on Warhammer. After enjoying his 40k take "Day of Ascension, I had a credit to spare on Audible and decided to take a flyer on it. I'll admit it was maybe 1 hour into it while I waffled on returning the book and getting my credit back, but decided to power through it and finished the book.

I'll say that this book is probably really geared towards those who are familiar with the Warhammer universe, and not just Warhammer, but Age of Sigmar. I've a passing familiarity with it thanks to playing some Vermintide, but honestly the nuances of races and armies and their special units have never been of much interest to me. Warhammer is a pretty exclusive hobby and if you're not equipped with time, talent for painting, and money, you'll never get into it. So a LOT of the description of the races, the stormcast, the lizard races, and the ratlings mostly went over my head. It took me a while to realize that the protagonist, the Starseer is actually a GOOD guy. Since my fantasy background is mostly in DnD, its a bit of a shock since I've been mostly seeing lizardmen as evil characters (thanks to the DnD trope that entire races can be evil....its changed a lot since, but that's when I was most involved with DnD).

Tachikovsky pulls it off for me at around 2 or 3 hours of the story and his usual flair of telling the story from different point of views really pays off here. He uses a different voice and tone for each faction, with the humans being the normal british english, the lizards taking more of a mystical or brutal take on the language, and the Skaven having your usual hiss and menace that the big bad guys must have. The plot is a bit of a treat too, taking your usual prophecy and ancient ruin combinations and having all 3 factions somehow being entangled in the fate of the prophecy. There's plenty of action amongst the factions, with the star of the cast being the Stormcast Eternal (paladins who get reforged each time they die and with each recasting they lose a bit more of their humanity) and his fight with the Lizard Champion (who coincidentally was the one who fought and killed an incarnation of the Stormcast. There are other elites of the human factions, the mage, the captain, the archer who each gets a telling in the tale. The lizard faction only gets the Starseer and the Champion, and the Skaven is mostly about the big bad.

The plot of it is a little convulated as most plots regarding prophecy can become, but all in all, the excuses it gives Tchaikovsky to get the factions into fights and other stuff is pretty interesting. The ending is not your typical Tchaikovsky happy ending, which came as a bit of a shock to me. Its still happy, but the twist is worthwhile to see.

All in all, never a bad read from Tchaikovsky yet. Recommended if you're a fan boy like me or just like the warhammer fantasy universe.
