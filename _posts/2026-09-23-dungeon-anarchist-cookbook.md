# The Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook
[Goodreads Review](https://www.goodreads.com/review/show/8962270123)

Book 3 of the DCC books and its all about train rides. This one I felt was the author overcomplicating things a bit. The basic tenents of the series is still there though, fast cuts, lots of action, mostly show, not telling. You start to see a lot more of the outside world in this one and also that the AI running the dungeon is starting to go a bit crazy.

This one also has a lot more teamwork happening in that Carl and his posse starts to recruit the rest of the humans. While some humans resist the teamwork aspect, most humans actually want to survive and believe that working together is the best course. Its slightly insane that only the humans can think of something like that, but I guess the author thought of that as well as later books explictly put teams against each other, including the inevitable PvP.

Some loose ends that weren't really loose ends gets cleaned up as well, and the characters have a lot more stakes put into them. I believe this is also the book where carl's ex and donut's (the cat protagonist) owner shows up, albeit in mostly a cameo and mostly for donut to tell her off. We believe our pets to be mostly loyal but at least in the case of this uplifted cat, she is well aware that she's to be sold off in exchange for a younger cat (she's a show cat so her value as a breeder is higher once she's past her prime).

All in all, I have to say this is probably one of the more fun series of books that I've read now that I'm 3 books in. There's constant pace and action, there's a clear line of action for the protaganists to take, and there's a bigger world out there. Its certainly grown on me quite a bit from the "oh, this is a hithchiker and mmorpg mashup" impression I had from book one.

there are a LOT of characters, but the cores ones are kept to a managable number. This one is a bit confusing because there's a lot of train line stuff that's hard to keep track of. You're mostly expected to go along for the ride because you're never given enough information to figure things out. You just have to know the plot is moved along and it sort of setups the scene for how the NPCs act and figure things out later on (which kinda makes them non-NPCs, but I digress).

Recommended as long as you start and power through book 1.
