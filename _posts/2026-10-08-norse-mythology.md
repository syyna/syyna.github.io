# Norse Mythology
[Goodreads Review](https://www.goodreads.com/review/show/8970173135)

Since we've been on a Neil Gaiman kick this year (me and my daughter), i decided to expand it further and go with Norse Mythology. This is partially one of those "i gotta be educating the kids" thing because I do not want her only knowledge of Norse Mythology to be from the Marvel comics.

I've previously listened to this a few years back, and this is part of a multi-year thing where sometime in the next 2 years, we'll be reading American Gods, and she'll have more of an appreciation for Odin, Thor, Loki, etc.

Anyway, back to THIS book. I think the important part of this book is that you have to listen to the foreword where Gaiman basically says NOBODY has a good library of stories outside the main characters in Norse mythology, so even though we have a whole cast of the norse pantheon, oral traditions and lack of written literature of the cast means only the stories on the main stalwart survived. Which is sad, because the Norse mythology seems more interesting than the old greek mythology that everyone knows (probably some of the interests is simply because its less familiar).

The rest of the book is a decent reconstruction of the stories that DO remain, and as usual you get a lot of Odin, Thor, and Loki. Secondary characters like Frey, Freya, the world serpent and a few of the Frost Giants are mentioned, but by and large you only get the big guys.

Still, the book was a success, the daughter is now both educated and also entertained by the masterfully retold stories. She now knows more about Norse Mythology than your average bear, and as usual, Gaiman's narration makes a good story even better.

Highly recommended.
