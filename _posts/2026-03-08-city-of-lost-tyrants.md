# City of Lost Tyrants
[Goodreads Review](https://www.goodreads.com/review/show/8358177756)

Another year, another book in Tchaikovsky's series The Tyrant Philosophers. I have to say that this series is becoming one of my favorite of his, even surpassing that of the "Children of" series. This book once again shows off another City in the Philosopher's universe, and once again, that city is becoming unduly influenced by the Pallenseen Sway in their quest to 'perfect' the world.

Also returning to the series, and the one constant character to the whole series is Jack and while not exactly the centerpiece of the book, is central enough that I want to say it really is about him. You need not have read the other books in the series to understand his motivations, though his interactions with the other folks in the book is so much the better.

This time, the city is built on a long dead crabgod, and the Sway is attempting to invade. This crab city has changed hands several times and its now the Sway's turn to get it back. The denizens of that city would rather keep its independence and to back that up, they have both terrain and two prior Pallaseen factions helping them. The bloodthirsty sea god and the pacifist healers. They've now more or less integrated into the rest of society in the sense that their secret is out and the Sway is trying to eradicate them in the quest for perfection.

Add some double agents, some commandos, and a host of undead serving perfection, and you've got your usual tchaikkovsky boiling pot story. There are numerous twists and turns and you get a bit of the distinction between this city and the rest of the cities we've visited thus far in the series. This one is more piratey/islandy in nature and all the characters are quite interesting, if not necessarily compelling.

I couldn't help myself but listen to the very end at relatively record speed though, so I have to say, if you've read the other 3 books of the series, this one's a no brainer. If you're interested in starting, I still have to recommend you start from Book 2, House of Open Wounds, as I consider that to be the highlight of the series thus far. None of the books are bad though, and all of them are more or less self contained. You miss out on some character fan service, but its not particularly important.

The ending of THIS book however, might change the rest of the Philospher's universe, but I thought that was happening as well in Book 2 and Book 3 merely went on the same timeline to neatly sidestep that particular issue. Needless to say, I have to say that the whole universe is quite good and worth reading.

Highly recommended.
