# Green City Wars
[Goodreads Review](https://www.goodreads.com/review/show/8825074182)

By this point, I think my reading diet is about 25% Tchaikovsky, 50% other fiction, and 25% non-fiction.  I KNOW its not that much, but it FEELS like it. Its probably because he's so prolific that he's sure to get out a book a season, so its probably more that I read one book of his per season more than anything else.  Just a random sunday observation....no science behind it.

In Green City Wars, he takes on the uplift concept that was quite the big hit in the 90s when I was in High school.  To this day, David Brin's Startide Rising still has a very strong resonance factor in my take on uplift ideas (the other uplift books weren't as good unfortunately).  I think Green City Wars might have supplanted Brin's take on uplift a little bit with a bit more science, and a lot more heart.  

Unlike lots of other Tchakovsky novels where perspectives shift and you get an "all sides" view of the storyline, Green City Wars essentially only follows one perspective, that of the Racoon protagonist.  The racoon is a private detective of sorts, and him and all the other uplifted animals were done so in the service of humanity's goal to be more green.  You can't be a green city and still maintain the trappings of modernity if you don't have things like garbage disposal, sewage, chefs, all the stuff that humanity wants...but don't want to pay for.  Enter the uplift strategy of taking animals like skunks, racoons, rats, hawks, etc and turn them into the unpaid slaves that humanity wants but don't want to recognize the ugliness of.

The hook by which the uplifted animals remain enslaved is the chemical that gives them the uplifting in the first place. Its probably a bit of a curse that what gives you enhanced conciousness is also what keeps you wanting that.  With that background in mind, the story begins with the racoon being tasked to find a mouse. Given that mice don't live long enough to be valued very highly (the uplift serum enhances intelligence, but not longevity), the racoon wonders why anyone would care enough about a mouse to spend the chemicals it costs (payment for service in the animal underworld is almost exclusively in ampoules of the uplift serum drug) for a detective.

Nevertheless, beggars can't be choosers, and the raccoon soon finds himself embroiled in a turf war, a succession war, a war between urban and suburban interests, and something that can change every enslaved animals life.

As per usual, Tchaikovsky's writing super power is world building and an unfettered imagination.  Green City Wars is an incredibly fun read (even if not very noir'ish) as the raccoon takes you to all the relevant places of the under-economy.  From mice who works in kitchens (cue ratttoutie) and parrots that are half crazed, and dogs that are un-uplifted and controlled by cats, the entire scenario is crazy and oh so fun.  Almost every urban animal is represented and you just have to wonder what the archetype of each animal represents.  Its not quite split down the racial lines, and you get a distinct grasp of the buildling geography by the mid way point of the book.

The story also has a very satisfactory happy ending that's a bit of a twist from the usual happy ending.  Although now that I've read both of Tchaikovsky's Warhammer novels (Day of Ascension and Starseer's Ruin), you realize that not all happy endings are equal.

All in all, highly recommended and I hope he continues to have more stories in this world.  Its a very different take from his other uplift series (The Dogs of War universe), and I find myself relating more easily with the characters in this universe.
