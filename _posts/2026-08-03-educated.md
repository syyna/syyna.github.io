# Educated
[Goodreads Review](https://www.goodreads.com/review/show/8825067141)

This is one of those books that I thought I wouldn't enjoy reading since I've never been an apple fanboy. I used to actively smirk at folks who buy what I considered over priced hardware and inferior software. This is even as my work places gradually moved over more and more to Mac OS and Mac hardware for my daily drivers, so while i was semi-forced to appreciate the apple hardware and software ecosystem, I was and am still firmly in the Android camp.

This showed up as something I read from 8 years ago and only just realized that its actually my brother borrowing it on his libby =)

Upon finishing it, I decided I needed to write the review if only because for the first time in my life, I had a concrete example of what family inflicted trauma looks like.  We all have our own stories, but this is the first time I read about someone's self-documented (she kept a lot of journals and a diary) and where the trauma spanned both physical and mental and over decades.

The book documents her childhood to until about 2017 or so (the book was published in 2018, but you figure it takes at least a year) so from her birth to about when she was early 30s.  She grew up in a mormon family who believed in Home Schooling and that the government was evil.  She suspects that her dad has bi-polar disorder later on in her life, but it does explain the paranoia she feels from her dad in the early years.

The book mostly goes into describing her family in the type of detail that I'd suspect was fabrication if not for the fact that she was apparently a dedicated diarist. She has 6 siblings and to be honest, keeping track of them was pretty hard the entire book.  The only sibling you don't have to keep tarck of is Shawn, who was her number 1 traumatizer, and Tyler, her favorite siblig.  All the other siblings don't factor much in her life except for specific instances.  

Tara grew up on a scrapyard, run by her Dad and pretty much the whole family except the Mom worked on it.  The mom was persuaded by the dad to become a herbalist and midwife.  The number of accidents that happened on the scrapyard are I.N.S.A.N.E and makes you appreciate things like OSHA and child labor laws.  

The book then goes into the title, which is how she was home schooled and what that means.  I think a LOT of details were skimped here, because even though the kids were not traditionally schooled, they still came out being able to read, write, do mathematics.  What I got the gist of, is that they mostly missed out on the non-important pieces of school, like how to take a test, and what homework is, and the other trappings of traditional school.  She goes into how when she took the ACT, she didn't know how to fill out a bubble answer form.  Or later when she goes to college, what a blue answer book is for, or that there were assigned reading homework.  

The fact that she got a 28 on her ACT (out of a possible 36, placing her in the 91st percentile) is testament that even if she did not receive knowledge of how to take tests, she had no problems studying for it and acing it.

Her college track record was similar.  She had gaffes in school and in everyday life, stemming from her lack of exposure to others, but once she gets going, she really gets going.  the fact that her other siblings also were similarly high performing leads one to believe her family is pretty gifted, if very unorthodox and that they were not all that deprived from actual education.  I do wish the memoir would document more details about how she went through college, but it seems like her entire family is full of dedicated folks who can really do anything once they put their mind to it.

The rest of the book documents her estrangement from her family and really educated ME on what family induced trauma looks like.  Her sister, Audrey, tries to call out her brother shawn for having tortured her mentally and physically, and  enlists the authoress to do so as well.  Given that the first half of the book was about the trauma inflicted by Shawn, this was a no brainer.  They tried to enlist their mom as well, and she was supportive.

However, once the dad was involved, he basically shut down the whole operation to call out shawn and asks for unreasonable proof.  This eventually led to the capitulation of Audrey and a mini betrayal of the authoress from said sister.   She gives up on trying to get some sort of accountability from the the parents and evantually accepts that she's no longer part of the family, though that took a lot out of her.  

I guess the takeaway here I got from the book was "no matter how battered you are, you always try to go back".  I always wondered why battered wives would always stay with their abuser husbands and this book documents the psychology of someone stuck in that situation.  Its the return to comfort, of the good times, because no abuser abuses all the time and the victim can always convince themselves that the good times outweigh the bad.  Or they gaslight themselves.  The authoress shows in great detail how all of these happened to her, because no matter how bad it gets, she never really thinks of moving out until her college experience and even after the estrangement, she still fantasizes about ways to get back into the family good graces without losing herself.

It is this insight that leads me to think this is a great book because I definitely felt educated about much more than this persons' life.  It IS hard reading, hence the 4*, not hard because it was badly written, but hard because its never easy reading about someone's abused child and adulthood.

Still, highly recommended.
