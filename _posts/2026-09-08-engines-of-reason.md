# Engines of Reason
[Goodreads Review](https://www.goodreads.com/review/show/8913453988)

The sequel to the Elder Gods. Its been a while since I read it, but one of Tchaikovsky's specialty is refamiliarizing you with his previous works. The world is now changed from the events of the last book, and we are now in the age where there are more than just the one offworld wizards. He has trained the main character from the previous book, and she's now a technomancer out to act in the interest of her home country.

She meets a new warlord, one who's also a technomancer, and one also backed by what appears to be another offworlder.

As usual, the best part of Tchaikovsky's books is the world building and this is no different. The wizard from the last book is now older, and more science is now given out more freely to the natives. He's also become less withdrawn from the world and although he refuses to do certain things (like subjugate the nation he resides in, or help them subjugate the neighbors), he's pretty much firmly in the world.

The world is much more fleshed out as well, from the space faring folks the wizard is from, to the actual native world he now resides in. You get a much stronger sense of geography as well as the tensions that those geography brings.

The conflict leaves a bit to be desired, simply because so much of it does not happen in the physical world, but its not really that bad either, just you don't expect a cyber battle in your typical science fiction meets fantasy trope.

The resolution is also quite unexpected, but does have the Tchaikovsky happy ending.

Still, as a short read, its great and serves as more than a simple palate cleanser. Highly recommended if you liked The Elder Gods.
