# Use of Weapons
[Goodreads Review](https://www.goodreads.com/review/show/8436649137)

This is a book that I must have first read over 20 years ago. My brother introduced the Culture novels to me, but for whatever reason I bounced hard off Consider Phelbas and The Player of Games. On a hail mary, I tried Use of Weapons and its the book that ultimately hooked me to the Culture series. Its the type of book that has been living rent-free in my head as the concepts and ideas presented within it was so mind boggling at the time that I read it.

Well, fast forward and 20 years later, even knowing what I know, the ideas are still equally mind boggling. The writing is as I remember, dry, sardonic, full of the wit that the protagonists and characters are known for. The utopian Culture vs some backwater star system that struggles against what the Culture wants to do, uplift the backwater culture into something more akin to the Culture.

The story follows that of the Contact Division agent Cheredine Zwalkawe. He's a bit of the specialized agent that the Culture uses from time to time when they need to get their hands dirty but also maintain plausible deniability. There is some backstory covered in other books about WHY the Culture wants to do things this way instead of just forcing their Culture on the indigenous population, but the best framework is probably the same reasons why a super power like the USSR or the USA have the CIA or KGB too.

In any case, the story has a two timeline structure, one going forward in time, and one going backwards in time. Its a little confusing at first, but once you get used to it, its pretty easy to track which timeline you're on in any part of the narrative. Unlike a lot of other books who use a similar type of split timeline narrative, BOTH parts of the narrative are fairly compelling.

The story almost exclusively tracks the exploits and shenanigans of Cheardine and it would have been a bore if the character was simple or a bore. He is neither. He's a combination of James Bond, Ethan Hunt from Mission Impossible, and any other type of super agent super smart suave character that you can think of.

The forward timeline story is fairly straightforward and just establishes what a badass Cheradine is, so the real gem is the backwards timeline where you can see how this person becomes such a bad ass.

The moment you find out why the book has the title it has is when you recognize the genius that is Iain M. Banks. I cannot say more without spoiling the moment, but all the parts that lead up to it was done perfectly. The moment you realize what is done and how it is done, still haunts some headspace of mine where "Use of Weapons" both in the literal and figurative sense.

Highly recommended, even after 20 years and knowing the plot, the book is still powerful and impactful. The best introduction to the Culture series if I had to recommend some place to start.
