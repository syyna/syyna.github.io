# Neverwhere
[Goodreads Review](https://www.goodreads.com/review/show/8926287306)

Seeing as how I was on a roll with Neil Gaiman's book, I decided to plow straight into more of the books that I knew he wrote but never read. Neverwhere is one of Gaiman's earlier books and is told much in the vein of The Graveyard Book. Except instead of a secret society of Jacks and supernatural beings, its more in the vein of the Overworld and the Underworld. If you're a londonite you'd be right at home as its pretty much described as London Above, and London Below. The London above is what you'd expect if you've ever visited London. Super fast paced, everyone ignoring you, and everyone is out to go someplace or see someone. London Below is...where everything you know about london is just about there, but not quite. The folks are grungier, they live in sewers, or dumpsters and the such. They have access to some of the tube, but mostly the forgotten tunnels and lines, and everyone is just a wee bit off. Described as the people and places that normal people have forgotten, London below is both familiar enough and strange enough that you never quite get the finger of it, but always have that "hmm, i think i've seen that before" feel.

The book follows the travels of Richard Mayhew, who is mostly a normal boring business man, and through one coincidental meeting with a folk from the London Below, becomes embroiled in the machinations of angels, of assassins, of bodyguards, and of one special Marquis.

Its an adventure tale much like The Graveyard book, but where certain things are just glossed over, here the adventures are put into great details. From the first meeting with the mysterious Door, to the favor Richard has to do to get Door out of his life, to his falling of grace from London Above, to finally accepting that he has to live through, survive the trials of being a denizen of London Below before he might get to be part of London Above again.

There is a bit of mystery here, as you try to tease out who exactly set Door on her journey, and how everything ties together, but this isn't a mystery book and everything is explained when Gaiman decides to let you know. Things don't always end up how you expect, but that's part of the reason to keep reading.

On the scale of great Gaiman books, I'd say this is better than Anansi Boys, but worse than American Gods, and not as strong as his short stories in general. But still an excellent read.

Highly recommended.
