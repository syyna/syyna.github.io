# American Gods
[Goodreads Review](https://www.goodreads.com/review/show/8895537076)

I don't know how many times I've seen American Gods, either on book stores or on the library shelf and just walked away simply because the title did not appeal to me. Never mind that Neil Gaiman was hugely influential in my teenage years, never mind that his movies, both animated (Coraline) and live action (Stardust) were big hits, never mind that his Miracle Man series was very disappointing. Wait, ok that last one didn't fit.

But I was running out of reading material and haven't read his other short stories like The Graveyard Book and The Ocean at the End of the Lane, I decided to dig into American Gods.

And by Gods, I was kicking myself for not reading it before for something as silly as THE TITLE.

The title was even quite apt, if I was being extra harsh on my younger self. In any case, this is another tour de force as good as the best of his Sandman stories. It follows the travels of Shadow, who lost his wife just a week before he is released from jail, for a crime of passion that he committed, and regretted. His release from jail is the last time that his life is seemingly normal, as he's accosted by pantheons of Gods, both new and some forgotten and some long forgotten. At first, the usual happens, he doesn't believe whats happening, but as he accepts his fate (for lack of a better word) and goes along for the ride, he finds unexpected origins and stories and even influence.

This is one of those books that I think one has to read for spoiling it should be a crime. The pacing is a little slow to start, but once it starts, it goes really really fast. Interspersed with the main plot are a bunch of side plots and by my count, there was no loose threads left untied at the end of the book. Everything was fully tied up.

Even the epilogue was incredibly banging.

So yes, don't be like my foolish self and not read it just because the title turns you off. The places are brought to live very lovingly and the city, people, and fables all fit to a tee.

One of Gaiman's best works. You'd be sorry if you don't read it. I know I am.

Highly highly recommended.
