# The Ministry of the Future
[Goodreads Review](https://www.goodreads.com/review/show/8584466159)

I had to double check, but I'm pretty sure this is my first Kim Stanley Robinson novel.

Its a near future climate disaster book and the first chapter is probably the one that gets quoted the most. Its a scarily depiction of what happens when the climate gets too hot and folks starts dying.  The indian subcontinent is going to suffer first because that's the hot and humid band and then the rest of the novel gets into what the rest of the world gets into it.

I get that its science fiction, and the idea that after millions die from climate related change is possible, but given that all the hurricanes, floods, wildfire and blizzards don't lead to change, its hard to believe that a heat related death of a city will lead to ANY change.

So yes, lets keep on reading to see how this may happen.  And yes, this is where the FANTASY/FICTION portion comes in.  The UN or some such appoints a Ministry for the Future, whose charter is to advocate for those who have not yet been born.  They have some small power and some small money, and the rest of the book mostly talks about what this Ministry tries to do, and succeeds or fails at.  

The REAL agent of change is NEVER identified but heavily hinted about who is invovled, which is that basically terrorists terrorizes the rest of the world into not using fossil fuels.  Airplanes gets bombed, boats gets bombed, and the perpetrators never get caught.  This level of fiction to me is so ludicrous that I almost stopped reading the book halfway through.  When billions of world trade is involved, its really ridiculous to me that the perps will not be accosted for.  The terrorits have the ability to bomb planes and the rest of the westernized nation just sits there and doesn't try to do anyting about it.  

In any case, the world leads to a happier place becuase now you can't really fly, and everyone has to take sailboats like in the good old days.  India is heavily said to lead this change since its one of their cities that paid the price of the first heat climate apocalypse, but that is largely lionizing what altruistic and forward thinking folks can do and say.

Its true that fast action needs to be taken to secure the future of the human race, but if there's one thing humans are good at, its that the needs of what we need today will always override the blurry tomorrow.  If humans were good at protecting the future, we wouldn't do self destructive things to OURSELVES like smoke, drink, or gamble.

So yes, science fiction, but i'd almost coin the word hope-fiction instead.  I'd say read the first chapter and skip the rest of the book.  would rate 2.5 stars if i could, but good reads doesn't let me do that.
